THE SPICE ISLAND

Zanzibar Holidays

Pair three or four safari days with a week of reef, sandbank, and Stone Town alleyways — the classic Tanzanian combination.

6.1553°S, 39.1922°E

Stone Town

A UNESCO old quarter of coral-stone buildings, carved doors, and spice-market alleys.

5.7333°S, 39.2833°E

Nungwi Beach

The island's liveliest north-tip beach — dhow-building yards, sunset bars, dive schools.

5.7833°S, 39.2833°E

Kendwa Beach

Unaffected by tide swings, so the beach stays swimmable all day — quieter than Nungwi.

6.2667°S, 39.5000°E

Paje Beach

East-coast kitesurfing hub with a wide shallow lagoon and a slower pace than the north.

6.2833°S, 39.3667°E

Jozani Forest

Home to the endemic Zanzibar red colobus monkey, found nowhere else on Earth.

6.1167°S, 39.2333°E

Prison Island

A short boat ride to snorkel and meet the island's population of Aldabra giant tortoises.

6.1500°S, 39.1667°E

Nakupenda Sandbank

A sandbar that appears at low tide, reachable by dhow for a beach-lunch day trip.

5.7500°S, 39.9500°E

Mnemba Island

The island's best reef — a protected marine conservation area popular for snorkeling and diving.

THINGS TO DO

Beyond the beach chair

Spice Farm Tour

Walk through working clove, nutmeg, and vanilla plantations with a local farmer as guide.

Kizimkazi Dolphin Tour

Early-morning boat trips to swim with wild bottlenose and humpback dolphins.

The Rock Restaurant

Dinner on a coral outcrop just offshore at Michamvi — book the tide-dependent boat transfer ahead.

Sunset Dhow Cruise

A traditional wooden sailing dhow along the west coast, timed for the evening light.
