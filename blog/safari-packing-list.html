The Packing List We Actually Give Our Guests

May 28, 20267 min readPlanning

Most safari packing lists are copied from each other and padded out to look thorough. This is the shorter, more specific version we actually hand guests before departure.

Clothing: fewer items, right colors

Neutral tones — khaki, olive, sand, muted green — are the standard advice, and it's correct: bright colors and pure white can spook wildlife and show dust badly. The detail most lists skip is that dark blue and black should be avoided specifically around Tarangire and the Katavi/Tabora corridor, where tsetse flies are drawn to dark colors. You don't need dedicated "safari brand" clothing — quick-dry hiking or travel clothes in the right palette work fine.

  • 2–3 short-sleeve shirts, 1–2 long-sleeve (sun and evening mosquito protection)
  • 1 warm layer — early morning game drives in an open vehicle are genuinely cold, even near the equator
  • A light rain jacket if you're traveling April–May or November
  • Comfortable closed shoes for camp; sandals for lodge downtime
  • A wide-brim hat and sunglasses — the equatorial sun is stronger than it looks on an overcast day

Health and comfort

  • Broad-spectrum sunscreen and lip balm with SPF
  • DEET-based insect repellent (30%+) — reapply after washing hands, which happens often on safari
  • Any personal medication in carry-on, plus a basic kit: rehydration salts, antihistamine, motion sickness tablets for bumpy roads, and any anti-malarial your doctor has prescribed for the trip
  • A reusable water bottle — most camps provide filtered or bottled water refills

Speak to a travel clinic 4–6 weeks before departure about malaria prophylaxis and yellow fever requirements — Tanzania requires proof of yellow fever vaccination if you're arriving from a country with risk of transmission.

Gear that's actually worth the suitcase space

  • Binoculars — one pair per two people minimum; birding and distant sightings are where these earn their keep
  • A dry bag or large ziplock for camera gear on dusty roads or Zanzibar boat transfers
  • A headlamp — most tented camps have limited lighting after dark
  • Power bank — charging outlets in remote camps can be solar-limited and shared
  • Universal plug adapter (Tanzania uses UK-style Type G sockets)

What you can leave at home

Guests consistently overpack: multiple pairs of jeans (heavy, slow to dry, too warm for game drives), hardshell suitcases (soft duffels pack better into safari vehicles and small aircraft for fly-in trips), and elaborate camera setups they don't already know how to use. If photography is a priority, bring a zoom lens you're comfortable operating quickly — a 100–400mm equivalent covers most sightings.

If you're adding Kilimanjaro

This needs a genuinely separate kit — insulated layers, a 4-season sleeping bag, proper trekking boots already broken in, and trekking poles. We provide a dedicated Kilimanjaro packing list once your climb is booked, since the requirements are specific enough that a generic safari list won't cover them properly.

If you're adding Zanzibar

Reef-safe sunscreen specifically (standard sunscreen is now restricted at some marine park sites), a light cover-up for modest dress in Stone Town, and water shoes for the rockier stretches of reef at low tide.

If in doubt, pack less. Almost every guest who overpacks tells us the same thing on day three: they wish they'd brought half of it.
