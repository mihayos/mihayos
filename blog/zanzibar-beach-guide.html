Zanzibar Travel Guide: Which Beach Fits Your Trip

April 3, 20267 min readZanzibar

Zanzibar isn't one beach — it's several very different coastlines stitched onto one island, and picking the wrong one for your trip is the most common planning mistake we see.

The tide problem nobody mentions upfront

Much of Zanzibar's east coast has a dramatic tidal range — at low tide, the water can retreat several hundred meters, leaving a wide flat of exposed sand and seaweed rather than a swimmable beach. This isn't a flaw in any particular resort; it's the geography of the east coast generally (Paje, Bwejuu, Jambiani). If "swim right off the beach any time of day" is non-negotiable for you, that points you toward the northwest coast instead.

Nungwi — lively, north tip, minimal tidal swing

The most developed beach town on the island: dive schools, sunset bars, dhow-building yards you can watch in action, and a wide range of restaurants. Because it sits at the island's northern tip, tidal swings are far less dramatic than the east coast, so the beach stays usable most of the day. Best for travelers who want some nightlife and activity options within walking distance.

Kendwa — Nungwi's quieter neighbor

A short taxi ride from Nungwi, with the same minimal-tide advantage but a noticeably calmer atmosphere and fewer permanent structures crowding the sand. This is where we send honeymoon and couples itineraries when guests want beach quality without Nungwi's liveliness.

Paje — the kitesurfing coast

East coast, wide tidal lagoon, and genuinely excellent wind conditions roughly December through March and June through September. If kitesurfing (or watching kitesurfers) is part of the appeal, this is the obvious choice — but go in with the tide schedule in mind rather than expecting an all-day swimmable beach.

Stone Town — not a beach trip, but essential anyway

Zanzibar's UNESCO-listed old town: coral-stone architecture, ornately carved wooden doors, a working spice market, and centuries of Swahili, Arab, Persian, and Indian trading history layered into one dense old quarter. Almost every itinerary we build includes at least one full day here, usually at the start or end of the beach stretch rather than as a standalone base.

Beyond the beach

  • Jozani Forest — the only place to see the endemic Zanzibar red colobus monkey
  • Prison Island — a short boat trip for snorkeling and resident giant tortoises
  • Kizimkazi dolphin tours — early-morning boat trips off the south coast
  • Spice farm tours — walking tours of working clove, nutmeg, and vanilla plantations
  • Mnemba Island — the island's best reef, a short boat ride from the northeast coast

Ask what someone actually wants from their beach days — swimming, diving, nightlife, or quiet — before booking a coastline, not after.
